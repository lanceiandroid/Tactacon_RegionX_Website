The people behind the places

Our History & Culture

Northern Mindanao's identity comes from its many communities, changing history, and connection to both the land and the sea.

A short background

A brief history

Long before modern provincial borders were drawn, the area was home to Indigenous communities and Moro communities with their own languages, customs, and ways of life. Coastal settlements later grew during the Spanish colonial period, while trade and travel connected communities around the region.

Over time, migration from other parts of the Philippines helped shape the population and languages heard across Northern Mindanao. Farming also became important to the region's economy, especially in Bukidnon, while Cagayan de Oro developed as a major center for trade, transport, and services.

Today, Northern Mindanao includes both busy cities and rural communities. Cebuano is widely spoken, but the region is not culturally uniform; Indigenous and Moro traditions remain important, and local histories differ from place to place.

Travel respectfully

Local customs matter.

When visiting cultural events or ancestral communities, ask before taking photos, follow community guidance, and support local makers and vendors when possible.

Things to know

Cultural highlights

01

Festivals to know

  • Kaamulan Festival — Bukidnon; celebrates the province's Indigenous cultures and community traditions.
  • Lanzones Festival — Camiguin; a thanksgiving celebration connected with the lanzones harvest.
  • Higalaay Festival — Cagayan de Oro; the city's annual fiesta celebrations and community events.
  • Kuyamis Festival — Misamis Oriental; highlights local identity and the province's coconut heritage.
02

Food and traditional crafts

  • Pastel — a soft, sweet bun associated with Camiguin.
  • Binaki — a steamed corn cake wrapped in corn husks, popular in parts of Mindanao.
  • Local seafood and grilled dishes — common choices in coastal towns and city markets.
  • Woven textiles and beadwork — traditional arts made by some Indigenous communities; designs and meanings vary by group.
